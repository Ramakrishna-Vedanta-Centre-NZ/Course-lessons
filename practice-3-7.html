The Breath Lever and Three Nervous-System States

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

The lesson calls breathing “one of the body’s slowest rhythms” and treats the breath as a low-frequency reference rhythm (§3). Here a pacer offers the lesson’s four in, six out beside an ordinary pace, while a toy model shows in words what the lesson says slow breathing does. Then you read six made-up scenes on the lesson’s map of three nervous-system states.

About the toy model and 0.1 Hz

The trace is invented to draw the lesson’s picture; it does not measure anything. The lesson says that at about six breaths a minute “your heartbeat synchronizes” and heart-rate variability increases, and adds: “The variation is not disorder. The variation is health” (§3). So at four in, six out the toy trace swings more, not less, and its swings become smooth and even. Real responses differ from person to person.

In the lesson’s equation, ψexp(t) = [ψₓ(t) ∗ h(t)] · G(t) · ψₘ(t) + n(t), “the body and prāṇa set the amplitude G(t) directly, and they influence every other term as well” (§5).

Not a warm-up

“Swami Vivekananda insisted that breath regulation (prāṇāyāma) is not a preliminary exercise, a warm-up before the ‘real’ meditation” (§1). And the breath is within reach: it “is one of the few autonomic processes you can voluntarily regulate” (§8.2). This is why, in the lesson, “prāṇāyāma precedes dhāraṇā” (§3).

Part II

Three states: a map, not a diagnosis

Scored: first pick counts

Six short scenes, all made up. For each, which of the lesson’s three nervous-system states does the scene describe? The lesson draws on polyvagal theory (Stephen Porges) and reads its three states as the three guṇas. On this page that is labelled the lesson’s correspondence. It is a map for reading the lesson, not a way to place yourself or anyone else.

The lesson adds: “The full meaning of sattva extends beyond the nervous system, but the nervous-system layer is real and accessible” (§4).

Quiet is not the same as calm

Two of the scenes are quiet, and only one of them is calm. In shutdown “the system does not stop processing; it continues to vibrate, but heavily and weakly” (§4), with low gain; in the ventral state gain is “stable” (§4). Lower gain alone is not the goal.

None of these states is a fixed label for a person. The lesson describes a nervous system that “has the flexibility to shift between states as needed” (§3), and names slow breathing as one way toward the ventral state (§4).
