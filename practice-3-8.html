Two Ways of Knowing

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Every thing is met twice: once as it is felt, and once as it is understood. Both are vṛttis, formed in the same field. Then there is a third way, which does not go through words at all. Here the three are set side by side in the notation the module has built: the object-wave ψₓ(t), the attention wave ψₘ(t) and the resultant R(t) from Lesson 3.2, and the stored past h(t) from Lesson 3.3.

Felt sense and understanding

Everyone has both. The felt sense is what it is like. The understanding is what it is about. Pick an example, then look at each lane on its own or together. The bell is the plainest case, hunger is inward, and a friend's words are relational.

Stored saṁskāra, h(t)

The filter of what was learned before. The doctor's training, the musician's ear, a shared history: each is h(t).

The words themselves

A second object-wave, ψₓ′(t), arriving by testimony and not by your own contact with the thing.

Inference

Links this felt sense to other cases: a cause, a course, a remedy.

The felt sense is first-hand, but not faithful by default

It arrives with the ahaṁkāra's tilt already on it. The envelope may be thin or flickering. You have the experience and not yet the understanding of it.

Understanding is structured, but second-hand

It reaches the thing through words and inference, and it passes through h(t). The lesson calls the two modes complementary: “One provides the map, the other provides direct access to the territory” (§7).

Part II

Why saṁyama knows differently

Practice: not scored

Everyday knowing reaches the object through words, concepts and ideas. In samādhi, where saṁyama is complete, the mind meets the object itself: Δf = 0, and the standing tilt of self-reference is released (§4, §5). This is a teaching picture, not a measurement: the object's fine structure stands for its particular character, and a concept keeps what is shared across many objects.

The crystal teaching: Yoga Sūtra 1.41

kṣīṇa-vṛtter abhijātasya iva maṇer grahītṛ-grahaṇa-grāhyeṣu tat-stha-tad-añjanatā samāpattiḥ (1.41)The transparent crystal

“When the fluctuations have become thin, the mind, like a transparent crystal, takes on the form of whatever stands near it (knower, knowing, or known). This is absorption (samāpatti)” (§10). In the course’s terms the mind does not disappear: it becomes a perfect instrument, transparent and matching the frequency of its object (§10).

Different modes, not rival answers

Aparā vidyā is “essential for practical life: navigation, relationship, learning skills, solving problems. Not to be rejected, but recognized as one mode among two” (§6). Parā vidyā is the higher knowledge “because it reveals the ground in which all difference-based knowledge appears. Not opposed to logical thinking but prior to it” (§6).

In this course’s terms, this unity-based knowing is parā vidyā, the knowing by which the Imperishable is attained (Muṇḍaka 1.1.5): “When Δf = 0, the beat stops completely: this is parā vidyā, the perfected resultant, samādhi” (§11). Lesson 3.2 calls the perfected resultant samādhi with a remaining object-pole, and both lessons place Self-Recognition beyond it: “Self-Recognition goes one step further, and belongs not to the equation but to the one who sees it” (§11).
