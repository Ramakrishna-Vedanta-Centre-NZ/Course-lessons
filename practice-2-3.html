Claim it or hold it

Choose an event, then choose how it is held. The wave is the lesson’s Module 2 image of a gain amplifier (§5.2): its shape stands for the content, its height for the gain. It has no numbers and measures nothing.

The content
How it is held

Two people, the same feedback (§5.2)

Tagged as core to identity

I am my work

Ahaṁkāra amplifies the signal enormously: praise becomes inflation (“I really am excellent”), criticism becomes injury (“I am failing, I am not good enough”).

The role held more lightly

work is something I do, not something I am

The gain is lower: the feedback is received as useful information, processed, and let go.

The feedback is identical.

Two faces, one function (§4)

“I am worthless”“I am superior”

Held lightly is not indifference

Held lightly, the feedback is still received as useful information, processed, and let go (§5.2). And a working “I” remains: the ego is not an enemy to be hated. It is a provisional organising structure. A healthy spiritual psychology aims at a functional ego with reduced rigidity (§8).

Insight alone is often not the end of it

In the last case, Sam could see that the point was fair. This is why insight alone is often insufficient for change. The ego’s defences must also soften. (§6)

And the grooves can change: identity can change, through the same mechanism in reverse. New patterns of observation, new ways of holding experience, and new relationships with self-reference can gradually create different grooves. (§5.4)
