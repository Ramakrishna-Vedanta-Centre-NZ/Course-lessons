Practice · Lesson 5.5

Knowledge, Solitude and Community

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Lesson 5.5 asks what changes when a wave of the mind is seen as a wave, rather than as “me”. Here you will watch a wave cross the sky of awareness, examine the assumptions beneath it, tell the three stages of knowing apart, see how solitude and good company shape the field, and look at what makes the ground ready.

Parts II and III are scored: your first pick counts. Parts I, IV and V are practice and are not scored.

Part I

Wave, or me?

Practice: not scored

Awareness is drawn here as a still sky. A wave of the mind crosses it. Choose a wave, then choose how to meet it: by identifying with it, or by asking what it is passing through. The bar shows the weight of the filter h(t), as a simple model.

Who is doing the discriminating?

The Pratyabhijñā lens in the lesson says: When the practitioner discriminates the transient from the permanent, it is not the small individual mind doing the discriminating from outside the equation. And on letting go: willed renunciation usually strengthens what it pushes against. (§2.2 and §4) The practitioner consents. The Śakti operates. (§4)

Examine the assumptions

The lesson says the standing tilt still carries assumptions: I am this body; I am this set of memories; I am the one who has these problems; I am the one who must achieve this outcome. (§2.3) Mananam takes them one at a time. For each of three, pick the observation that loosens it, and a weight lifts from the filter. Your first pick counts.
