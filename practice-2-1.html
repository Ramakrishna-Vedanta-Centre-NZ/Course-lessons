Three Movements

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Lesson 2.1 describes how one Consciousness-Energy comes to appear as an individual through three linked movements: localization, limitation and identification. Here you will step through them with the lesson’s picture of sunlight and a window, then sort the lesson’s own examples of the two meanings of “I”.

Five steps, in the lesson’s order (§2). At each step the panel shows what appears and what does not change. The light is drawn the same at every step: Limitation is not the same as division (§8).

What does not change

Switch identification off and on. The person, the body-mind, the window and the narrowed channel stay exactly as they were.

This switch illustrates the lesson’s distinction. It is not a state to be reached by pressing a button.

The lesson’s own summary line (§7.2)

C∞ → P(B-M) → Λ → I → J

Universal Consciousness-Energy appears centred through a particular body-mind system. That centred mode appears under contraction. The contracted, centred mode is identified with. And the result is the individual experiencer.

Not a different substance

The sense of self is not made of some alien substance. It is Consciousness-Energy itself, appearing in a contracted and conditioned mode. (§3)

Individuality as manifestation need not be a problem. (§2.3) Your individuality is not a mistake. It is a manifestation. But it is not the whole of what you are. (§6)

Part II

Two meanings of “I”

Scored: first pick counts

Ana is made up; her day follows the lesson’s own example (§3). Sort each card: is it one of the shifting self-images, or what remains constant through them?

Card 1 of 5

Pick one of the two.

The costume and the wearer

The individual “I” is the universal “I” wearing a particular costume. But when the costume is mistaken for the wearer, confusion begins. (§3)

Sorting is not rejecting. Distinguishing the two is the beginning of liberation, not because the personal self must be destroyed, but because it can finally be seen in its proper place: as an instrument and expression of something deeper (§3).
