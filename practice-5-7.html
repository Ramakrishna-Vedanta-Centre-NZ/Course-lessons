Practice · Lesson 5.7

One Signal, Two Readings

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Lesson 5.7 says the temporal and the spectral are two readings of one thing, and that lowering the noise lets what was always present register. Here you will read one signal two ways, watch the noise fall, look at the seven stations as the lesson draws them, sort what science can and cannot reach, and look at the staircase.

Part I

One signal, read two ways

Practice: not scored

The lesson says The two readings are different views of the same thing: the temporal view shows what is happening now; the spectral view shows the structure that is generating the now. (§2.2) Here a signal is built from three steady waves. Change any of them and watch both readings change together.

Teaching picture, not a measurement. The frame around the picture is not one of the three waves.

Part II

When the noise falls

Practice: not scored

The lesson says that when anṛta is high, the spectral ground is occluded, and that as temporal Ritam is established the signal-to-noise ratio rises. (§2.3) Below, the slow wave is a steady hum. Lower the noise and watch it become discriminable in both readings.

The honest turn: the hum is a stand-in

The hum is only a picture. The lesson says the substrate is Not a deeper layer of the signal but the awareness in which the signal is being read at all. (§2.2) The practitioner does not produce the substrate; the substrate has always been there. (§2.3) No amplifier could make it louder, because it is not one of the waves.

What the metaphor cannot carry

A standard Fourier transform is reversible, and it acts on a finite signal. The Ātman is not a finite signal. It is the awareness in which all signals arise. The lesson says the metaphor is faithful up to {9}, and that beyond it the mathematics points beyond itself. (§3.5)

The lesson’s own example is a practitioner noticing the gap between thoughts at a traffic light. (§2.3)
