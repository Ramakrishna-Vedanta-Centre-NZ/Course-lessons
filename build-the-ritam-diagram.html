Build the Ritam Diagram

Nine points on a circle, three small number patterns, and three names: tamas, rajas and sattva. Build the diagram yourself, see how doubling differs from trebling, lift it into the torus, and find out why the three guṇas are strands to balance, never valences to rank.

I. Build the diagram

Seven short steps. At each one you do the arithmetic yourself, so that the closing of the loop is something you watch happen, not something you are told. The next step opens when the activity is done.

    II. Lift the diagram

    Doubling kept every number inside its own family. Trebling does something different. Pick a number, then double or treble it. Before you press Treble, say to yourself where you expect to land.

      The two-dimensional diagram is a display. The full architecture is three-dimensional and lives in one form, the torus: a surface made by revolving a small circle around a larger one. Each strand has its place on it. Switch each one off and see what the torus loses.

      A note on humility

      We are not claiming that every natural system is exactly toroidal, or that the torus is the only organising form in nature. Spheres, helices, fractals, lattices and branching trees each have their own rationale. The claim is that the torus is one organising form, and that it appears with striking frequency in self-sustaining living systems. The Ritam Diagram is a useful display of one important architectural pattern, not the unique theory of everything (§8).

      III. Three strands, not three valences

      In some popular treatments, tamas is “bad”, rajas is “neutral” and sattva is “good”, and the aim is to escape tamas, moderate rajas and cultivate sattva. The lesson calls this a deep mistake. Test it yourself: drag any strand toward zero and see what happens.

      Hold the framing

      The diagnostic question is never which guṇa is good and which is bad. It is which is over- or under-expressed for this person, in this body, at this stage of life, right now (§9). Three strands, all indispensable, all to be balanced, never three valences to be ranked.
