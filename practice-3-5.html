Practice · Lesson 3.5

Three Dials and a 2x2

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Lesson 3.5 says that attention is the mind’s own wave, ψₘ, and that it has three parameters practice can reach, each set in a different place. Here you will turn the three dials against a fixed object wave, place four short made-up cases on a grid of arousal and steadiness, and then trade depth against responsiveness with the bandwidth of attention.

Part II is scored: your first pick for each case counts. Parts I and III are practice and are not scored.

Part I

Three dials

Practice: not scored

The object wave ψₓ stays fixed at its frequency fₛ. The attention wave meets it, and their resultant carries a carrier and an envelope (§1). Turn each dial on its own and watch which part of the picture it changes. Each dial is labelled with what sets it in the lesson.

The middle of the frequency dial is the object’s own frequency. Neither side of it is better: what the lesson asks is how well fₘ matches this object. The attention wave is drawn weaker than the object wave, so the envelope never falls to nothing.

The attention wave in the lesson’s notation

ψₘ(t) = Aₘ · cos(2πfₘ·t + φₘ)

The attention wave enters the equation scaled by a slowly varying gain, G(t), the strength with which the body and prāṇa drive it, so that what meets the object is G(t) · ψₘ(t). (§1) The page draws only this meeting. It is a model, and it does not measure anyone’s attention.

Arousal is not steadiness

You can have high arousal but scattered attention (an anxious person who is scattered despite being activated), or low arousal but steady attention (a tired person who remains focussed on what matters). (§1) Try it above: a stronger wave still beats if fₘ is off the object.

Part II

Arousal and steadiness

Scored: first pick counts

Two of the dials make a grid: arousal, the gain G that the body and prāṇa set, and the steadiness of fₘ, which manas holds. Four short cases, all made up. Two follow the examples the lesson gives in §1; two are added for this page. Place each one on the grid.

Case 1 of 4

Rows: arousal, the gain G (body and prāṇa). Columns: steadiness of fₘ (manas).

Pick one of the four.

Two dials, not one

Arousal and steadiness are set in different places: the gain by the body and prāṇa, the frequency by manas (§1). The grid places moments in made-up lives, not people, and no corner of it is a verdict. The gain can be shifted through breath work and body practice (which Lesson 3.7 addresses) (§1).

The rectangle’s width is the bandwidth and its height is the time the event takes to register. Its area does not shrink as you move the dial: BW · Δt ≥ constant (§2). The page draws the limit case, where the product equals the constant.

Narrow, and then effortless

The page cannot draw the next step, so it is left to the lesson’s words: Over time, something shifts. The bandwidth remains narrow, but the grip loosens. The frequency no longer wavers. This transition is meditation (dhyāna): the bandwidth is narrow but the engagement is effortless, like oil pouring smoothly from one vessel to another. (§2)

Nor is focus the whole story: A mind that has trained itself to focus intently but is focused on its own conditioned beliefs is not more free; it is more imprisoned. (§7)
