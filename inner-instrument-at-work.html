The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Lesson 2.2

The inner instrument at work

The mind is not one thing. It is one inner instrument, the antaḥkaraṇa, with four functions: manas, buddhi, ahaṁkāra and chitta. Here you will follow a single ordinary moment through all four, then learn to locate a difficulty in the function where it belongs, and finally see the four as one.

I. Build the moment

Every moment of experience passes through the four functions in turn, and what is left behind shapes the next moment. Place each function where it acts.

II. Which function is disturbed?

“My mind is a mess” is too vague to act on. The classical model asks a better question: which function is disturbed, and what does that function need? Read each short case and choose the function where the difficulty is mainly located. Real life mixes them, so the feedback also says which others join in.

III. One river

The four are not four organs sitting side by side. They are four modes of functioning within one inner instrument, like currents at different depths and speeds in a single river.

Hold both truths

For the work of self-observation, the distinction between the Self and the instrument is indispensable: it lets you stop being every passing disturbance. At a deeper level, the lesson adds, it is a teaching tool and not the final truth. The instrument is not a second thing. It is the One Existence appearing in the form of mind, as waves are the ocean in motion.
