Practice · Lesson 2.7

Trace the Moment

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Lesson 2.7 follows one ordinary moment, a brief remark at a family gathering, through the whole inner instrument, and shows how a few words have become an emotional event that occupies your inner life for hours. Here you will trace the moment step by step, run the loop again on what was stored, and then go through it once more, noticing.

Part I is scored: your first pick at each step counts. Parts II and III are practice and are not scored.

Part I

Trace the moment

Scored: first pick counts

You are at a family gathering. An older relative makes a remark, something about your career, your appearance, or a choice you have made. The words are brief. The tone is ambiguous. It could be concern, criticism, or a clumsy attempt at affection. (§1)

This is the lesson’s own scene, and the remark is left general. If family gatherings are painful for you, read it as any brief remark from anyone, or simply follow the diagram.

At each step you see the lesson’s description of what happens, with the function’s name left out. Which function is at work?

Step 1 of 4

Pick one of the four.

Manas detects and orients; buddhi interprets and assigns meaning; ahaṁkāra claims and amplifies; chitta stores the impression (§1, Steps 1 to 4). Part II follows what happens next.

Part II

The loop closes

Practice: not scored

Step 5 of §1. The conversation has moved on, but the moment has not. Press That evening to replay it on stored material, as many times as you like, then The next gathering.

Nothing new has been said. Only the stored impression is there.

The loop as it stood after the gathering.

Not a punishment

This recursive loop is the psychological mechanism of what the spiritual traditions call saṁsāra: the wheel of conditioned existence. It is not a punishment. It is not a cosmic conspiracy. It is pattern-reinforcement, operating automatically within the inner instrument. And it operates automatically unless awareness intervenes. (§2)

The full cycle, as §2 states it

Input → L₂a/Manas (detect, compare, orient) → L₂b/Buddhi (interpret, determine meaning) → L₂c/Ahaṁkāra (self-tag, claim as “mine”) → L₂d/Chitta (store as saṁskāric trace) → Feedback into L₂a (biasing future manas) and L₂b (biasing future buddhi) → Next cycle begins from conditioned ground

What you bring

Lesson 1.1 said experience is a meeting of ψₓ (what is actually in front of you) and ψₘ (what you bring to it). Now: What you bring is the accumulated output of the inner instrument’s processing over a lifetime. It is the saṁskāras stored in chitta, the habitual interpretations of buddhi, the self-referential patterns of ahaṁkāra, and the attentional biases of manas. It is not one thing. (§3)

Changing what you bring means changing the field itself, which is why genuine transformation requires sustained practice, not just momentary insight. (§3)

Part III

Notice

Practice: not scored

The same moment once more. At any step, press Notice. Nothing is scored, and nothing here is a test of how well you notice.

The loop is drawn just as before.

But who was doing the watching?

If you can observe the loop (if you can see manas reacting, catch buddhi assigning meaning, feel ahaṁkāra claiming ownership, and sense the impression forming in chitta) then you are not only the loop. There is something in you that is aware of the whole process without being caught in it. (§4)

That something is what the traditions call the witness, the sākṣī: the awareness that sees but is not seen, that knows but is not known as an object. Whatever this page could draw would be one more thing seen, so the diagram stays as it was.

This recognition does not happen all at once. It does not require you to stop being a person or to abandon your life. It begins simply, with the capacity to observe the loop. Each time you observe the loop without being caught in it, you are resting, even briefly, as the awareness that the loop has been obscuring. And that resting is the beginning of everything that follows in this course. (§4)
