Groove Garden

Chitta is the mind-field in which experience leaves traces. Here you will first sort the three words the lesson holds together, then watch one small trigger meet one stored impression again and again, and finally compare two ways of imagining change: erasing the field, or replacing what is reactive in it.

I. Field, seed or plant?

The lesson relates three terms: chitta, saṁskāra and vṛtti. Take each statement below and place it where it belongs. A few statements describe something else entirely.

Chitta, the fieldThe retentive ground. Not destroyed, and not to be.
Saṁskāra, the seedThe stored impression, latent beneath the surface.
Vṛtti, the plantThe active formation that grows when a seed is stirred.

§3: “chitta is the field, saṁskāra is the seed, and vṛtti is the plant that grows from the seed when conditions are right.” The seed is stirred by present experience, memory, desire, fear or attention. It does not rise at random. Part II lets you stir one.

§5.2 The pattern-continuity loop
    Read it as the lesson’s two equations

    ψexp(t) = f[ψx(t), h(t), ψm(t)]

    What you experience depends on what is happening, ψx(t); on the state of the mind-field, h(t); and on where attention is directed, ψm(t). Here the input is the same every time, and attention is not varied. What changes is the field, h(t).

    h(t+1) = h(t) + new impressions − weakening or transformation

    • A trigger: a new reactive impression is added, and little is weakened.
    • A moment of insight: almost nothing is weakened.
    • A session of practice: a clearer impression is added, and a reactive one weakens a little.

    A qualitative picture of the lesson’s model. It does not measure anyone.

    Hold this gently

    A deep groove is not a personal failing. The lesson frames persistent patterns as retained impressions, formed under particular conditions, that are now doing what impressions do (§8). “The person is not weak or broken.”

    III. Erase or replace?

    Imagine the field holds six impressions: three that are reactive, distorted or fear-based, and three worth keeping. Choose how change is to be imagined, then act on it.

    Side by side

    Chitta is the basis of both bondage and growth

    The same mind-field that retains painful patterns also retains spiritual insight, noble tendencies, devotional feeling, refined discrimination, the fruits of practice, the capacity for compassion and moments of genuine peace (§7). Chitta-śuddhi is understood “not as punishment or self-correction, but as the natural process by which the mind-field becomes progressively more transparent to the light of Consciousness” (§9.4).
