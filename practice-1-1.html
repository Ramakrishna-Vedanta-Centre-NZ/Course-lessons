Practice · Lesson 1.1

Given or Brought?

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Every perception is a meeting of two things: what is given, ψₓ, and what you bring, ψₘ. Here you will sort ordinary moments into the two, hold the same given while the person changes, and then watch the two waves of the lesson become one ripple.

Part I

Sort the moment

Scored: first pick counts

Read each statement and decide whether it belongs to what is given or to what is brought. Tap a button on the statement. Your first pick on each statement is the one that counts; if it is not right you can still try again to see why. There are four moments, and you can take them in any order.

Given · ψₓ

What you could write down as bare description: who said what, what happened, what was visible or audible. Descriptive, not interpretive (§13). These are bare descriptive facts, not a claim about objective reality.

Brought · ψₘ

Your mood, the memories it touches, what you expect, fear or hope, the labels and history you carry (§10, §13).

    Brought ψₘ

      Two things the lesson says, held together

      Experience comes first, before it is named or judged (§9). And what feels like direct observation is always, at least partly, interpretation (§6, §14.2). So this sorting does not claim that a pure, unmixed given can ever be had, and “given” here means bare descriptive facts (§13), not objective reality. It is a practice of description: afterwards, you separate what you could simply state from what you added. In the living moment the two are already one ripple (§10).

      Nor is ψₘ a fault to be removed. Memory, mood, expectation and meaning are part of how the mind knows (§10), and thought is “valuable, but partial” (§6).

          What this shows, and what it does not

          The same given can be met by different people and become different situations. That is the lesson's explanation of why “two people in the same room can have entirely different experiences” (§10). It does not say that one of them is wrong. The invitation of §14.4 is gentler: once you can see that a difficult experience is the meeting of an outer ψₓ and an inner ψₘ, it becomes workable, because you have a part in it.

          Part III

          Two waves, one ripple

          Practice: not scored

          The lesson's picture (§10, Figure 1.1.b): one wave comes in from outside, another arises from your own inner movement. Where they meet they combine into a single ripple, and that ripple is what you experience as “the situation”. It is not the original wave.

          Whose ψₘ?

          A picture, not a measurement

          The shapes above are drawn for illustration. There is no scale, no unit and nothing measured: the lesson says only that the two combine into one ripple. How the picture is made precise comes later, in Module 3 (§3).

          One question stays open, and this page does not try to close it. If every perception is ψₓ mixed with ψₘ, what is the knowing in which the mixing appears? The lesson says this “cannot be answered by more thinking” and that it has to be looked into (§11). That looking is the work of Lesson 1.2.

          Your own turn comes on paper: the two-column experiment of §13, done with a real reaction of your own. A page cannot do that looking for you.
