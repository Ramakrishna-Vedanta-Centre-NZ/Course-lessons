Practice · Lesson 3.4

Lower the Floor

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Lesson 3.4 says that noise hides a signal without removing it, and that practice lowers the noise floor so that what was always there can come through. Here you will lower the floor under a faint, steady signal, see what the lesson says becomes perceptible as it drops, and then name four kinds of noise in four short scenes.

Parts I and II are practice and are not scored. Part III is scored: your first pick in each scene counts.

A difference in noise, not in worth

Neither of these people is morally superior or inferior. Neither has a “better mind” or “worse mind.” They simply have different noise floors. It is a difference in system configuration, not a character flaw. (§2)

What becomes perceptible

The slider has no numbers on purpose. The order is the lesson’s; the points at which each comes through are drawn by the page, and are not stages to reach.

Beyond the last setting

No setting of the slider stands for the last step of §9, so it is given here as the lesson’s text: At the deepest stillness, the fundamental freedom of Consciousness is no longer something approached or attained. It is known by Itself, as Itself, in immediate presence. The Ātman recognizes the Ātman. This is not an achievement of the small self; it is the dropping away of the obscuration that made the small self seem to be all there is. (§9)

Practice does not manufacture peace, manufacture the witness, or manufacture Consciousness. (§9)

Features of an untrained system

All of these types of noise are present in the ordinary mind, in various combinations. They are not abnormal. They are simply features of an untrained system. (§4)

Lower the floor, do not fight the noise

An intuitive response to mental noise is to fight it: to suppress the thoughts, to resist the emotions, to force the mind to be still. A better approach is to lower the baseline noise through systemic practices: simplification of input, regulation of the nervous system, ethical living, and the repetition of what is wholesome. (§11)
