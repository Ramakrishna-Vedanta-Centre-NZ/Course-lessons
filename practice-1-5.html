Distinctions kept, forgetting undone

“The bondage does not arise from difference; it arises from forgetting the unity within the difference, from taking the slice for the whole” (§4). So the work of insight “is not to destroy Māyā” but to undo the forgetting. Food and poison stay different.

Not a verdict on anyone

“This is what suffering through Māyā actually looks like. It is not that the differentiation is wrong; the distinctions are real” (§4). The cards are made-up statements, not descriptions of any person, and nothing here is a diagnosis. A medical reading remains useful information: the lesson lists “Health must be distinguished from illness” among the working distinctions.

    Next month’s talk has not happened. This picture draws on the facts above, “drawing on templates from the past” (§6).

    Mira is made up. Here you set the leaning with a button; in life it is not a switch that anyone flips, and this page does not say how any person’s mind works or that anyone chooses their memories. It illustrates one point of the lesson: the same facts can be put together in more than one way, and the putting-together happens now.

    The suffering is entirely real

    “This does not mean the suffering is not real; the suffering is entirely real, because the construction is happening, and the brain is responding to it as if it were real. What it means is that the structure that generates the suffering is workable” (§6). The lesson’s own Try This (§8) is a gentle noticing, and it says to carry it “lightly”. If it feels too much, you can leave it, and speaking with someone you trust or a qualified professional is always open to you.
