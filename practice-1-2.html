Where the sorting stops

In every case the “I” in the sentence turned out to be something noticed. Then who noticed? The lesson does not answer with a thing: You can notice a thought. You can notice the I who is noticing the thought. But when you try to notice the one who is noticing the I-who-is-noticing, you find that you cannot get behind the observing. The observing is what is looking. It is not another thing being looked at. (§5)

So the observing is not among the things known, and this page has nowhere to put it: no column, no button. Whatever could be drawn on a page would be one more thing looked at. The sorting above only illustrates the argument; it does not find anything.

What the lesson offers instead is looking. Inquiry One: Do not try to find the something that is aware. Just notice that the noticing is happening. Inquiry Two: The point is not to answer with words. (§8)

And, as the lesson says plainly: You have not discovered that you do not exist. The empirical “I” (the particular person reading these words) is entirely real. If the observation does not land yet, that is fine. (§6)
