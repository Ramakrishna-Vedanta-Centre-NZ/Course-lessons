Saṁyama in Motion and the Antarāya Clinic

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Saṁyama is attention held on one object until the beat between knower and known is stilled. In Part I it is drawn in the notation of Lessons 3.2 and 5.3. Parts II to IV then take the lesson’s second movement: nine invented practitioners and the nine antarāyas, the four signs that arise with them and where the lesson says to begin, and one rise in the signal-to-noise ratio that dissolves them.

Nothing on this page is scored, and the page sends no result to your record. Every part is a learning aid that shows the lesson’s reasoning. None of it measures you or places you at a level.

Each slip pulls attention to another object and sets the practice back. An untrained mind slips often and loses a lot, so it keeps returning to pratyāhāra and dhāraṇā. A trained mind slips rarely, loses little and recovers fast.

The scale is exaggerated so beats are visible: the object sits at 5 Hz. The six rows under each stage are the columns of the Saṁyama Table in Lesson 5.3. Where the lesson states an endpoint (Δf = 0, Δφ = 0, R(t) = 2A cos(2π fs t)) the page follows it exactly. The motion between stages is a teaching picture.

Why the higher stages are preferred

More of the object arrives

While the beat runs, the envelope sweeps from full to empty and averages about 64% of full whatever the tilt. Once the beat stops, the tilt decides: the meeting delivers 2A·cos(Δφ/2). At Δφ = 180° nothing arrives. At Δφ = 0 the amplitude is 2A.

No flicker, no strain

The beat at |Δf| is the felt pulse of separation, the rhythm of reaching and missing. As Δf goes to zero the envelope stops fluctuating and the effort goes with it.

Truer content

The carrier sits at (fs + fm)/2. As fm comes to fs the content moves to the object's own rhythm, so what you know is what is there. ψm is also the wave you can train.

The same model, measured at each stage

Samādhi is {9}, not {0}

Saṁyama, however deep, is the highest reach of the manifest equation. The recognition that the awareness in which the equation appears is what one is, is a separate movement. It is not another stage of the meeting, and it is the subject of Lesson 5.7.

The nine, as the lesson’s table gives them

“Mixed” indicates an antarāya that can present with either tamasic or rajasic signature depending on the system; it is not a third guṇa-category (§3.5).

Not a failure of will

Each antarāya is a structural failure-mode: it names which component of the instrument is malfunctioning and how (§3.5). Read beside the Saṁyama Table, the Antarāya Table tells the practitioner what is going wrong; the Saṁyama Table tells them what comes right when the antarāya dissolves.

A learning aid, not an assessment. The practitioners are invented, and nothing here diagnoses anyone. Illness belongs first with a doctor; distress with a qualified professional or someone you trust.

What comes with it

Practice: not scored

YS I.31 names four signs that arise together with the distractions. Then §7 says where a failing Saṁyama has to begin. Work through both.

The four co-arising signs

§3.6 describes a practitioner like this: “sitting is uncomfortable, mood is low, the body will not settle, and the breath is short and ragged.” Tap one observation, then the sign it shows.

Four observations, four signs.

Choose an observation to begin.

These four signs arise with which antarāyas?

And should the practitioner work on each sign separately?

Where does this practitioner begin?

“The practitioner whose Saṁyama is failing cannot simply try harder. The failure has structural causes, and the structure tells us where to begin” (§7). Choose a case, read it again, and choose what comes first.

Rhythm prepares; attention crosses

“In both presentations, the principle is the same: Saṁyama begins where the rhythm-and-sound family of Lesson 5.2 has prepared the ground. Once the threshold is crossed, japa with artha-bhāvanam, Patañjali’s prescribed practice, is the natural entry into dhāraṇā” (§7).

A learning aid, not an assessment. It shows the lesson’s reasoning on invented cases and gives no personal advice. Illness comes first to medical care, not to practice.
