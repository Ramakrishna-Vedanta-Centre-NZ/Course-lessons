Practice · Lesson 4.6

What Are You Coupled To?

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Lesson 4.6 names three universal phenomena: resonance, entrainment and coherence (§4). Here you drive a swinging system past its natural rate, let a board of metronomes find one another, and then see what a group of oscillators does when it is coupled to a steady source and to an erratic one.

Parts I and III are practice and are not scored. Part II is scored: only its four questions count, and your first pick on each counts.

Part I

Past the natural rate

Practice: not scored

A mass hangs from a spring, and the top of the spring is moved up and down by a small, steady push. The system has a natural rate at which it most readily swings. Move the slider to change the rate of the push, or let the page sweep it for you, and watch how far the mass swings. The push itself is the same size at every setting.

The curve is worked out for this spring and mass; the dot is the swing the model is making at this moment, so it takes a few seconds to settle after you move the slider. The model has one spring and one mass and nothing else.

The lesson’s own picture

“Every physical system has a natural frequency, the rate at which it most readily oscillates when disturbed. Strike a wine glass and it rings at a specific pitch” (§4, Resonance). In the lesson’s analogy, a system driven at that rate “draws” on the field around it. The model shows only the swing growing near the match. It does not model a source, and the analogy is not scored.

Question 1 of 4

Pick one answer.

Coherence is not uniformity

“In a coherent system, the rhythms are not identical; they maintain stable phase relationships; each knows, so to speak, where the others are in their cycle. Coherence is not uniformity. It is coordinated diversity” (§4, Coherence).

Part III

What are you coupled to?

“Steady” means a rhythm that keeps one rate. “Erratic” means one whose rate keeps wandering. The source is not pulled by the group. This is a generic model: it does not model a person, a nervous system or any situation, and it takes no view on what anyone should couple to.

Neutral, and then where it leads

“The mechanism of resonance is neutral. It does not care what you couple to” (§4). “In every case, the system phase-locks with what holds it.” The lesson then goes on: “the direction of that flow depends entirely on what you are coupled to.” This page shows the first part only: the group follows whichever rhythm it is given. What each kind of source does to a living being is the lesson’s own account, and is not modelled here.

“Let a man lift his mind by his own Self alone; let him not lower his mind. The mind alone is one’s friend; the mind alone is one’s enemy.” Bhagavad Gītā 6.5, in the wording the lesson gives (§4).
