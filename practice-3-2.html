A struck bell sends out one steady pitch. That is the object-wave ψₓ(t). Your attention brings a wave of its own, ψₘ(t). What you hear is their sum, the resultant R(t), which is the vṛtti. Here you can hear it, and change it, then see which function of the inner instrument sets what.

Part II

Who sets what

Practice: not scored

The attention wave brings two things to every meeting: a frequency and a posture (§2). In the lesson’s mapping, each is the work of a different function of the inner instrument (§8), and all of them act at once.

fₘManas

sets the pitch of attention. The pitch slider in Part I is its work. A collected manas means a steady fₘ that can be tuned toward the object (§8).

ΔφAhaṁkāra

the standing tilt: the self-reference that angles every meeting toward “me and mine”. It is brought to the meeting, not produced by it (§4.2).

Δφ → 0Buddhi

corrects the phase and reads the carrier. In Part I, step 5, watch the fullness rise as it does (§8).

The identity behind the sound

A cos(2π fₛ t) + A cos(2π fₘ t) = 2A · cos(2π (fₛ − fₘ)/2 · t) · cos(2π (fₛ + fₘ)/2 · t)

Notation follows Lesson 3.2. The envelope runs at half the difference and is felt as a beat at |fₛ − fₘ|, written Δf (§1, §3). When fₘ = fₛ the sum has fullness 2A · cos(Δφ/2) (§4.2). The page uses equal amplitudes, the lesson’s teaching case.

Nothing new is made in the meeting

The swelling and fading you hear belongs to neither tone alone. Yet “if you analysed the combined sound with an instrument, you would find only the two original frequencies” (§1). The lesson reads this in two registers: superposition in the mathematics, superimposition (adhyāsa) in the philosophy (§4.3).

The perfected meeting, and what lies beyond it

When Δf and Δφ both close to zero, the resultant is one wave at the object’s own frequency, R(t) = 2A · cos(2π fₛ t): the object alone shining (§4.3). The lesson calls this parā vidyā, and “the high-water mark of the empirical equation: savikalpa samādhi, samādhi with a remaining object-pole” (§9). “But Self-Recognition lies beyond even this” (§9).
