The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Lesson 3.8

Two ways of knowing

Every thing is met twice: once as it is felt, and once as it is understood. Both are vṛttis, formed in the same field. Then there is a third way, which does not go through words at all. Here the three are set side by side in the notation of Lessons 3.2 and 3.8: the object-wave ψx(t), the attention wave ψm(t), the stored past h(t) and the resultant R(t).

I. Felt sense and understanding

Everyone has both. The felt sense is what it is like. The understanding is what it is about. Pick an example, then look at each lane on its own or together. The bell is the plainest case, hunger is inward, and a friend's words are relational.

What contributes to understanding

Buddhi

Reads the carrier of the felt sense and assigns meaning to it.

Stored saṁskāra, h(t)

The filter of what was learned before. The doctor's training, the musician's ear, a shared history: each is h(t).

The words themselves

A second object-wave, ψₓ′(t), arriving by testimony and not by your own contact with the thing.

Inference

Links this felt sense to other cases: a cause, a course, a remedy.

The felt sense is first-hand, but not faithful by default

It arrives with the ahaṁkāra's tilt already on it. The envelope may be thin or flickering. You have the experience and not yet the understanding of it.

Understanding is structured, but second-hand

It gives relations, predictions and a shared language, and it passes through h(t), so it is only as true as the stored pattern and the words. Neither replaces the other.

II. Why saṁyama knows differently

Everyday knowing reaches the object through words, concepts and ideas. Saṁyama meets the object itself, with Δf and Δφ both at zero. This is a teaching picture, not a measurement: the object's fine structure stands for its particular character, and a concept keeps what is shared across many objects.

What Patañjali says, read in the course's terms

śabdārtha-pratyayānām itaretarādhyāsāt saṅkaraḥ (3.17)Word, object and idea are mixed up

They are superimposed on one another (adhyāsa). Saṁyama on their separation unmixes them.

śabdārtha-jñāna-vikalpaiḥ saṅkīrṇā savitarkā (1.42)Ordinary absorption is still mixed

Even when attention rests on an object, word, meaning and knowledge are tangled together.

smṛti-pariśuddhau svarūpa-śūnyevārtha-mātra-nirbhāsā nirvitarkā (1.43)With memory purified, only the object shines

One reading in the course's terms: purified smṛti is h(t) no longer colouring the incoming wave, and “as if empty of its own form” is the standing tilt Δφ released.

ṛtaṁbharā tatra prajñā (1.48)Truth-bearing insight

Insight that carries what is. In the course's terms, the object alone shining: R(t) = 2A cos(2π fs t).

śrutānumāna-prajñābhyām anya-viṣayā viśeṣārthatvāt (1.49)A different object from testimony and inference

Because it reaches the particular (viśeṣa). Words and inference reach what is shared.

taj-jayāt prajñālokaḥ (3.5)The light of direct knowledge

With resonance established, the object is known by frequency-recognition, the same frequency recognising itself, and not by inference.

Different questions, not rival answers

For treating an infection, the doctor's understanding is the right tool: it is public, checkable and built from many cases. Saṁyama is superior in one respect only. It gives the thing itself, in its own particular nature, with no proxy, no filter and no self-reference in between. It also cannot be handed over in words, so it must be verified by those who practise it.

In this course's terms, this unity-based knowing is parā vidyā (Lesson 3.8), the knowing by which the Imperishable is attained (Muṇḍaka 1.1.5). Lesson 3.2 adds that even the perfected meeting still has two poles, and that Self-Recognition lies beyond it.

Notation follows Lessons 3.2 and 3.8. The explanation-side object-wave is written ψₓ′(t) to mark it as a second object, the words about the thing, entering through h(t).
