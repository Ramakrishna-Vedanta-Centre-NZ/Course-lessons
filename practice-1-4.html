Part I

Stability is the dynamic

Practice: not scored

Section 5 of the lesson says that a living body holds a steady state “not by stopping all activity, but precisely through continuous dynamic activity”. Here one made-up quantity is held near a set-point by many small corrections while small pushes keep arriving. Disturb it, then take away the adjusting, and then the set-point, and watch what each loss does.

What the three tries show

“Remove the movement and the stability collapses. Remove the stability and the movement becomes chaos” (§5). Here, with the adjusting paused the quantity is carried away by the pushes, and with the set-point removed the adjusting goes on but has nothing to hold. “The stability is the dynamic. The stillness is the movement, held in balance” (§5).

Illustrative only. This is one generic quantity with no units, and the time scale is chosen for the picture. It is not a model of the body: the lesson says “thousands of processes are adjusting, compensating, and restoring in every moment” (§5), and a single regulator is only a stand-in for that pattern.

Back to your own breath

The lesson says: “Most people, doing this honestly, find that the line cannot be drawn. The breath is one continuous event in which movement and stillness are not separate stages but two qualities of the same wholeness” (§8). The picture above only shows that, in a drawing, any line you draw is where you chose to draw it. The noticing is in the Try This, in your own breath, about thirty seconds, during ordinary activity.
