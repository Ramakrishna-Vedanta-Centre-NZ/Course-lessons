Strike the System

The Mind in the Light of Modern and Spiritual Sciences · Advanced

Strike a tuning fork once and it rings in its own way. The lesson asks you to look at the mind the same way: a glance, a word or a scent strikes it, and what follows is the mind’s impulse response, h(t). Here you will strike a model of it and watch its three features, then find those features in four short stories.

Part I is practice and is not scored. Part II is scored: your first pick in each story counts.

Dampers named in §2: each adds some damping here

§2 also names a calm environment and unhurried time. The helpers are labels for what the lesson describes, not amounts: the lesson gives no formula, and the page adds the same step for each.

Where h(t) sits in the lesson’s equation

ψexp(t) = [ψx(t) ∗ h(t)] + ψm(t)

The object wave ψx(t) is shaped by h(t), the impulse response that the saṁskāras held in chitta form, before the attention wave ψm(t) meets it. Their resultant is the vṛtti that is experienced, ψexp(t) (§13). This lab shows only h(t): what the system does when it is struck once, briefly (§1).

The trace is a model, drawn as a simple ringing that dies away so that its three features can be seen. It does not measure anyone, and the lesson does not say a mind is a fixed filter: it says the filter is reshaped by practice, “japa (repetition of a sacred name), breath regulation, ethical living, and inquiry” (§3), as a riverbed is re-carved (§8).

Damping is not numbness

Well-damped does not mean feeling less. “You feel your feelings fully when something triggers you, but the response is proportionate, and you settle relatively quickly” (§2). Try it: raise the damping and the reaction still rises. It simply stops ringing.

Part II

Which feature is at issue?

Scored: first pick counts

Four short stories, all made up. In each, someone is struck by a small input. Which of the three features does the story turn on?

Story 1 of 4

Pick one of the three.

Three features, one system

Two of these stories begin with almost the same remark. “The difference is not the comment itself. It is the settling time of the system” (§1). Settling time, overshoot and residual ripple are “features of your system’s impulse response, shaped by your history” (§1).

Noticing, not blaming

“Learning to notice them is not to blame yourself; it is to wake up to how your mind actually works” (§1). And the deepest damping, the lesson says, comes from seeing your own pattern: “Once you see the pattern, it begins to lose its hold” (§2).
